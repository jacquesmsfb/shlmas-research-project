# Related Studies

## RFID-Based Library Automation Studies

The application of Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology in library workflows has been extensively investigated due to its ability to improve circulation efficiency, inventory management, and transaction accuracy.

Engel (2017) conducted an empirical evaluation of RFID implementation in academic library collections, focusing on both operational efficiency and financial feasibility. The study utilized RFID transponders attached to library materials and specialized RFID readers integrated with centralized library management software. Through transaction time analysis, Engel demonstrated that RFID-assisted checkout procedures significantly reduced circulation processing time compared to manual card-based recording and barcode scanning. However, the study identified that implementation costs were substantially increased by the requirement of attaching individual RFID tags to every cataloged physical book copy. This limitation established item-level RFID tagging costs as a major barrier preventing adoption among institutions with limited financial resources.

Similarly, Shahid (2005) investigated the implementation of RFID-assisted circulation control and inventory management systems. The study examined the use of RFID readers to automate book checkouts, returns, and inventory auditing procedures. Results showed that RFID integration reduced human data-entry errors, improved inventory auditing efficiency, and generated more accurate digital borrowing records. However, the study also identified limitations related to the dependence on expensive hardware infrastructure and network requirements, which may create financial and technical challenges for resource-constrained educational institutions.

These studies demonstrate that RFID technology provides significant improvements over traditional manual library procedures by increasing transaction speed, improving data accuracy, and reducing administrative workload. However, the continued dependence on item-level RFID tagging and costly enterprise infrastructure highlights the need for alternative RFID approaches, such as the hybrid RFID model proposed in the Smart Hybrid Library Management and Automation System (SHLMAS), which aims to make RFID-based circulation more accessible for public basic education schools.

## Automated Library Management System Studies

Research on software-driven library management platforms emphasizes the transition from physical record systems to structured digital database environments.

Adewole et al. (2020) developed a web-based automated library management system designed to manage borrowing records, calculate due dates, and monitor student borrowing activities. The system utilized a structured SQL relational database and web-based interface to replace manual ledger recording. The researchers reported improvements in record accuracy, reduced dependence on physical documentation, and decreased administrative workload among library personnel.

However, a limitation identified in the study was the continued dependence on manual data input during transactions. Library personnel were still required to encode student information and book accession details into the system, which could create processing delays during periods of high library activity.

In a related study, Velasco (2019) examined the implementation of digital library record systems in Philippine public high schools. The research analyzed how centralized databases improved the organization of circulation histories and enabled more effective monitoring of book availability. While digital records improved information management, the study noted that transaction processing remained limited when users and library personnel were required to manually enter identification information.

These studies demonstrate that software-based library management systems effectively address issues associated with paper-based record keeping by improving data organization, accessibility, and storage reliability. However, software-only solutions remain constrained by manual encoding requirements. This highlights the importance of integrating automated identification technologies, such as RFID systems, to further improve circulation efficiency and minimize human input.

## Embedded System and Microcontroller-Based Automation Studies

Recent engineering research has explored the use of low-cost microcontrollers as reliable processing platforms for localized automation and Internet of Things (IoT) applications.

Suryana et al. (2020) developed an embedded transaction logging kiosk using an ESP32 microcontroller paired with an RC522 RFID reader module. The study evaluated the capability of the system to process RFID identification data and manage transaction records without continuous cloud connectivity. Findings demonstrated that the ESP32 provided sufficient processing capability, hardware stability, and storage capacity for localized automation tasks. The system was also capable of temporarily storing transaction records during network interruptions and synchronizing data after connectivity was restored.

Similarly, Kumar and Sharma (2021) developed a low-cost embedded identification and resource tracking system using an RC522 RFID module and an open-source microcontroller architecture. The study evaluated authentication performance, processing speed, and implementation cost. Results showed that the system provided reliable identification while maintaining a low-cost hardware structure. The researchers concluded that modular embedded systems provide practical alternatives to expensive industrial automation solutions.

These studies establish that microcontroller-based platforms, particularly ESP32 and RC522 RFID combinations, provide affordable and reliable foundations for educational technology prototypes. However, existing embedded systems have primarily focused on applications such as attendance monitoring and access control rather than complete library circulation workflows. This limitation presents an opportunity for SHLMAS to apply embedded automation specifically to public school library operations.

## Philippine Educational Technology Studies

The implementation of educational technology in Philippine public basic education is influenced by infrastructure limitations, financial constraints, and availability of technical resources.

The National Economic and Development Authority (NEDA, 2021) identified challenges affecting digital transformation in Philippine education, including limited ICT infrastructure, hardware maintenance difficulties, and inconsistent internet availability. Similarly, the Department of Education (DepEd, 2019) emphasized the importance of integrating ICT equipment and digital solutions to improve educational services and learning environments.

Despite these initiatives, many public schools continue to experience difficulties adopting advanced technological systems due to budget limitations and limited technical personnel. Velasco (2019) highlighted that many Philippine public school libraries operate under the supervision of teacher-librarians, where classroom teachers manage library responsibilities alongside their regular teaching duties. Because of these operational conditions, complex automation systems requiring extensive maintenance and specialized technical support may not be practical for public education settings.

These studies emphasize the need for affordable, simple, and sustainable technology solutions designed specifically for public schools. By combining low-cost RFID components, microcontroller-based processing, offline transaction buffering, and a hybrid RFID identification approach, SHLMAS addresses the technological and operational limitations identified in previous studies while providing a practical automation solution for public school libraries.
