# Related Literature

## Library Management Systems

A Library Management System (LMS) is an integrated information management tool designed to organize, process, and monitor essential library operations, including cataloging, patron management, circulation, and reporting (Asemi et al., 2010). Its primary purpose is to centralize information management, improve operational efficiency, optimize resource allocation, and provide structured access to educational resources.

Within school environments, an LMS serves as the operational foundation that connects students and faculty members with available physical and digital learning materials. It enables libraries to maintain organized collections while supporting efficient resource discovery and utilization (Asemi et al., 2010).

One of the most important components of any library system is circulation management, which oversees the movement of resources between the library and its users (Bansode & Periera, 2008). Circulation activities include student identification, book borrowing, returning, due-date monitoring, overdue tracking, and transaction record maintenance.

In many traditional school libraries, particularly in Philippine public basic education institutions, circulation procedures still rely on manual paper-based methods. These processes require students to manually record information such as names, identification numbers, book titles, and accession details through logbooks or index cards. Library personnel must then verify these entries against existing records, creating additional workload and increasing the possibility of errors (Department of Education [DepEd], 2011; Velasco, 2019).

Manual circulation systems present several operational limitations. Handwritten records are vulnerable to inaccurate entries, illegible handwriting, damaged documents, misplaced pages, and possible identity falsification. Furthermore, retrieving historical borrowing information or determining book availability requires staff to manually search through physical records, resulting in slower transactions and reduced library efficiency.

The transition toward digital record management addresses many of these limitations by replacing physical documentation with structured databases. Digital systems allow borrowing and returning transactions to be automatically recorded, timestamped, and linked to specific users and resources. This improves record accuracy, enables faster information retrieval, supports real-time monitoring of available materials, and allows administrators to generate usage reports for better decision-making.

For Philippine public schools, digital record management is particularly beneficial because many teacher-librarians manage library responsibilities alongside regular teaching duties. Automating repetitive administrative processes reduces clerical workload and allows school personnel to focus more on student assistance, literacy programs, and academic support.

## Library Automation

Library automation refers to the application of computer systems, specialized software, and electronic hardware to perform library operations that were traditionally completed manually (Singh, 2003). These automated functions include Online Public Access Catalogs (OPAC), computerized circulation systems, inventory monitoring, and digital user management.

The implementation of automated library systems improves operational efficiency, data accuracy, and workflow consistency. By replacing repetitive manual activities such as handwriting, record searching, and physical filing with electronic processing, automation reduces transaction time and minimizes human errors caused by fatigue or inaccurate encoding (Singh, 2003).

Modern library automation has evolved from simple database-based inventory systems into integrated hardware and software environments. These systems allow physical interactions, such as scanning identification cards or registering borrowed resources, to immediately generate corresponding digital records. This connection between physical library activities and digital databases enables faster, more reliable, and more organized circulation management.

Within the Philippine basic education system, automation supports the increasing demand for independent research and information access brought by the K to 12 curriculum under Republic Act No. 10533. Subjects such as *Practical Research 1*, *Practical Research 2*, and *Inquiries, Investigations, and Immersion* require students to access references, evaluate information sources, and conduct independent investigations.

However, manual library procedures often struggle to support these research requirements efficiently due to slow transaction processing and limited record management capabilities. Implementing automated circulation systems in public school libraries can reduce waiting times, improve resource accessibility, and align library operations with modern educational requirements.

## RFID Technology in Libraries

Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) is a wireless identification technology that uses electromagnetic fields to transfer information between RFID tags or cards and reader devices for identification and tracking purposes (Finkenzeller, 2010). Unlike barcode-based systems, RFID does not require direct visual alignment between the reader and the identified object, allowing faster and more flexible data transmission (Ayre, 2005).

A standard RFID system consists of three major components:

- **RFID Tags and Cards:** Electronic identifiers containing a microchip and antenna that store unique identification data or a UID (Finkenzeller, 2010).
- **RFID Readers:** Devices that emit radio frequency signals and receive identification information from nearby RFID tags.
- **Processing Systems or Microcontrollers:** Computing units that interpret RFID data, execute system logic, and communicate with databases (Kumar & Sharma, 2021).

In library environments, RFID technology automates borrower authentication and resource identification. Instead of manually writing transaction details, users can interact with the system through a simple tap-based process. The RFID reader captures identification information, verifies the user and resource details, and records the borrowing or returning transaction automatically (Ayre, 2005).

Compared with traditional circulation methods, RFID provides several advantages:

| Manual Logbook System | Barcode-Based System | RFID-Assisted System |
|---|---|---|
| Requires handwritten transaction records | Requires optical scanning alignment | Uses contactless identification |
| Vulnerable to recording errors and damaged documents | Requires visible and readable labels | Enables rapid tap-based transactions |
| Slow processing and manual verification | Faster but still requires scanning each item | Provides automated and efficient logging |

RFID-assisted circulation reduces manual encoding requirements, improves transaction speed, and increases record accuracy. Although commercial RFID library systems are effective, their implementation often requires expensive infrastructure and individual RFID tagging of every physical book, creating challenges for resource-limited educational institutions (Shahid, 2005).

## Low-Cost Technology Solutions in Education

The implementation of educational technology in public schools is often limited by financial constraints, infrastructure availability, and maintenance requirements (DepEd, 2019; National Economic and Development Authority [NEDA], 2021). While commercial library automation solutions provide advanced capabilities, their high costs make adoption difficult for many public schools.

Enterprise RFID library systems commonly require individual RFID tags for every book, dedicated scanning equipment, and specialized infrastructure. These requirements create significant financial barriers, particularly for schools operating with limited technology budgets.

To address these challenges, educational technology research emphasizes the development of low-cost, modular, and accessible systems using affordable hardware and open-source technologies (Fisher & Baird, 2005). Microcontrollers such as the ESP32 enable researchers and developers to create customized automation solutions by combining low-cost processors with modular electronic components.

The ESP32 is a low-power microcontroller with integrated wireless communication capabilities, sufficient processing performance, and multiple hardware interfaces suitable for automation applications (Suryana et al., 2020). When paired with components such as RFID reader modules, displays, and database systems, microcontroller-based solutions can perform automated identification and transaction processing at significantly lower costs compared with commercial alternatives (Kumar & Sharma, 2021).

These principles support the development of the Smart Hybrid Library Management and Automation System (SHLMAS). By utilizing affordable RFID components, microcontroller-based processing, and a hybrid identification approach, SHLMAS aims to provide a practical library automation solution suitable for public schools with limited financial and technological resources.
