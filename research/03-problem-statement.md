# Problem Statement

Despite the important role of school libraries in supporting education, many library operations are still performed manually through handwritten logbooks or spreadsheet-based record keeping. While these methods are functional, they often result in slow transactions, inaccurate records, increased librarian workload, and difficulty monitoring borrowed books.

Students may forget to return borrowed books on time, books may become misplaced or lost, and librarians must spend additional time manually updating records and preparing reports. Furthermore, students are often unable to determine whether a book is available without physically visiting the library.

These challenges demonstrate the need for a more efficient and automated library circulation system that improves transaction speed, inventory accuracy, and overall library management while remaining practical for implementation in secondary schools.

This study proposes the development of the **Smart Hybrid Library Management and Automation System (SHLMAS)**, an integrated platform consisting of a student web portal, self-service borrowing kiosk, smart return box, centralized database, and librarian dashboard. The system is designed to automate library circulation while maintaining librarian supervision and improving operational efficiency.

The study seeks to determine whether implementing SHLMAS can significantly improve library circulation compared with the school's existing manual process.

---

# Research Questions

## General Research Question

**Can a Smart Hybrid Library Management and Automation System (SHLMAS) significantly improve the efficiency and accuracy of library circulation compared to the existing manual process?**

---

## Specific Research Questions

This study specifically seeks to answer the following questions:

1. Can SHLMAS significantly reduce the average time required to borrow and return library books?

2. Can SHLMAS improve the accuracy of library inventory records compared with the existing manual system?

3. Can SHLMAS reduce the number of overdue and lost books through automated tracking and reporting?

4. Does the implementation of SHLMAS improve librarians' efficiency in managing library circulation and inventory?

5. Does SHLMAS improve students' ability to search, reserve, and access library resources?

6. What is the level of user satisfaction among students, librarians, and teachers regarding the usability, functionality, and effectiveness of SHLMAS?

7. What strengths, limitations, and implementation challenges are identified during the deployment of SHLMAS in a school library environment?

---

# Research Hypotheses

## Null Hypothesis (H₀)

There is **no significant difference** in the efficiency and accuracy of library circulation between the existing manual library process and the Smart Hybrid Library Management and Automation System (SHLMAS).

---

## Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)

There is a **significant improvement** in the efficiency and accuracy of library circulation after implementing the Smart Hybrid Library Management and Automation System (SHLMAS) compared with the existing manual process.

# Operational Definitions

To ensure consistency throughout the study, the following terms are defined operationally:

**Library Circulation** – The complete process of borrowing, returning, reserving, and tracking books within the school library.

**Efficiency** – The ability of the library system to complete borrowing, returning, and inventory management tasks using less time and effort.

**Inventory Accuracy** – The degree to which the digital records correctly reflect the actual status and location of books.

**Overdue Book** – A borrowed book that has not been returned on or before its assigned due date.

**Borrowing Kiosk** – A self-service station where students authenticate their identity and scan books to record borrowing transactions.

**Smart Return Box** – An automated return station that scans returned books and immediately updates the central database.

**Hybrid System** – A system that combines both software (web portal and database) and hardware (kiosk and return box) into one integrated library management ecosystem.
