# Scope and Delimitations of the Study

## Scope of the Study

This study focuses on the design, development, implementation, and evaluation of the **Smart Hybrid Library Management and Automation System (SHLMAS)** for a secondary school library. The system is intended to automate and improve the library circulation process while maintaining librarian supervision over library operations.

The study covers the development of an integrated system consisting of the following components:

- Student Web Portal
- Librarian Dashboard
- Self-Service Borrowing Kiosk
- Smart Return Box
- Centralized Database
- Analytics and Reporting Module

The Student Web Portal allows students to:

- Search books by title, author, subject, or grade level
- View real-time book availability
- Reserve unavailable books
- View borrowing history
- Receive rule-based book recommendations

The Librarian Dashboard enables librarians to:

- Add, edit, and remove book records
- Manage student accounts
- Monitor borrowing and returning transactions
- Generate reports and analytics
- Approve borrowing requests for restricted materials when necessary

The Borrowing Kiosk allows students to:

- Authenticate using their assigned QR code
- Scan a book's QR code
- Record borrowing transactions automatically
- Generate due dates

The Smart Return Box automatically:

- Detects returned books through QR code scanning
- Updates the central database
- Closes borrowing records
- Marks books as available for future borrowing

The study also evaluates the effectiveness of SHLMAS by comparing it with the school's existing manual library circulation process.

The evaluation focuses on the following indicators:

- Borrowing transaction time
- Returning transaction time
- Inventory accuracy
- Number of overdue books
- Number of misplaced or lost books
- Librarian workload
- User satisfaction
- Overall library circulation efficiency

Data will be collected through:

- User surveys
- Direct observation
- System-generated reports
- Borrowing and returning records
- Time measurements during transactions

The respondents of the study include:

- Students
- Librarians
- Teachers

# Delimitations of the Study

To ensure that the project remains feasible within the available resources and research period, several limitations are established.

The study is limited to:

- One participating secondary school library.
- Printed books available within the selected library.
- QR code technology for student and book identification.
- A local network or school-hosted server for system operation.
- Prototype hardware consisting of a tablet, ESP32 microcontroller, QR scanner, and smart return box.

The following features are **outside the scope** of this study:

- RFID implementation (future enhancement)
- Face recognition authentication
- Artificial intelligence or machine learning recommendation systems
- Mobile application development
- SMS or email notification services
- Cloud-based synchronization between multiple schools
- Online payment or library fine management
- Integration with external school information systems
- Support for multiple libraries within one installation

Although these features may improve the system in future versions, they are not necessary for evaluating the primary objectives of this research.

---

# Assumptions of the Study

This study is conducted under the following assumptions:

- Students will use their assigned accounts responsibly.
- Librarians will accurately maintain the library database.
- QR codes attached to books remain readable throughout the evaluation period.
- The local network remains operational during testing.
- Users receive basic orientation before using the system.
- Borrowing and returning procedures follow the workflow established by the researchers.

---

# Limitations of the Study

Several factors may affect the results of this research but are beyond the researchers' control.

These include:

- Internet or local network interruptions.
- Hardware failures such as damaged QR scanners or tablets.
- Damaged or unreadable QR code labels.
- Changes in library policies during the research period.
- Limited testing duration.
- Limited number of respondents.
- User errors during system testing.

These limitations are acknowledged to provide proper interpretation of the findings and to guide future improvements of SHLMAS.

---

# Boundaries of the Research

The study focuses specifically on evaluating whether a **Smart Hybrid Library Management and Automation System** can significantly improve the efficiency and accuracy of library circulation compared with the existing manual process.

The research evaluates the effectiveness of the proposed workflow rather than the superiority of a specific programming language, database system, hardware platform, or software framework.

Therefore, the findings of this study are intended to demonstrate the value of workflow automation in school libraries, regardless of future technological implementations or upgrades.
