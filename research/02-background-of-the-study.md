# Background of the Study

## Introduction

School libraries are essential learning environments that provide students with access to books, references, and other educational resources that support academic growth and independent learning. An effective library system ensures that these resources remain organized, accessible, and readily available to students and teachers whenever they are needed.

Despite the increasing availability of digital technologies, many secondary school libraries continue to manage book circulation through handwritten logbooks, paper records, or spreadsheet-based systems. While these methods are inexpensive and familiar to librarians, they become increasingly inefficient as the size of the library collection and the number of borrowers grow.

Modern educational institutions are gradually adopting information systems to automate administrative tasks and improve operational efficiency. However, many existing Library Management Systems are designed for universities or large organizations and often require expensive subscriptions, specialized hardware, or complex configurations that may not be practical for public or secondary schools.

This situation highlights the need for a practical, affordable, and scalable library management solution specifically designed for the needs of school libraries.

---

# Current Situation

Based on observations of traditional library operations, borrowing and returning books remain largely manual processes. Students typically record their information in logbooks, while librarians manually update borrowing records and monitor due dates.

Although functional, this workflow presents several operational challenges.

Common issues include:

- Time-consuming borrowing and returning procedures
- Students forgetting to return borrowed books
- Difficulty tracking borrowed and misplaced books
- Inaccurate inventory records due to human error
- Labor-intensive report generation
- Lack of real-time information regarding book availability
- Increased administrative workload for librarians

These challenges reduce the overall efficiency of library operations and may discourage students from utilizing library resources.

---

# Technological Opportunity

Recent advancements in web technologies, embedded systems, QR code identification, and database management have made workflow automation more accessible than ever before. These technologies enable educational institutions to simplify repetitive administrative tasks while improving data accuracy and operational efficiency.

QR code technology, in particular, offers an inexpensive and reliable method of identifying both students and books without requiring specialized hardware. Combined with web-based information systems and centralized databases, QR codes can significantly reduce transaction times while minimizing human errors in record keeping.

Furthermore, affordable microcontrollers such as the ESP32 have made it possible to develop embedded automation devices—including self-service kiosks and smart return boxes—that can communicate with centralized databases in real time.

These technological advancements create an opportunity to redesign traditional library workflows rather than simply replacing paper records with digital versions.

---

# The Proposed Solution

To address these challenges, this study proposes the development of the **Smart Hybrid Library Management and Automation System (SHLMAS)**.

Rather than functioning solely as a digital record-keeping application, SHLMAS integrates multiple software and hardware components into a unified ecosystem designed to automate library circulation while maintaining librarian supervision.

The proposed system consists of:

- A web-based student portal for searching books, checking availability, viewing borrowing history, and reserving books.
- A self-service borrowing kiosk that allows students to authenticate themselves and borrow books using QR code technology.
- A smart return box capable of automatically recording returned books and updating inventory records.
- A centralized database that synchronizes transactions across all system modules.
- A librarian dashboard that provides inventory management, analytics, report generation, and administrative controls.

This hybrid approach combines the convenience of automation with librarian oversight, ensuring that library operations remain both efficient and secure.

---

# Research Gap

Although numerous commercial Library Management Systems are available, many primarily focus on digitizing existing manual processes rather than redesigning the complete library circulation workflow.

Additionally, few systems combine:

- A web-based student portal
- A self-service borrowing kiosk
- A smart return box
- Centralized real-time inventory management
- Automated reporting and analytics

into a single modular platform specifically designed for secondary school environments.

This study seeks to address this gap by developing a practical hybrid system that is affordable, scalable, and tailored to the operational needs of school libraries.

---

# Significance of the Proposed Study

The Smart Hybrid Library Management and Automation System aims to improve both operational efficiency and user experience by automating repetitive circulation tasks while maintaining accurate digital records.

Specifically, the system seeks to:

- Reduce borrowing and returning transaction times.
- Improve inventory accuracy through automatic record updates.
- Reduce the number of overdue and misplaced books.
- Minimize librarians' administrative workload.
- Improve students' access to library resources through real-time information.
- Provide meaningful analytics to support library management and decision-making.

Rather than evaluating software functionality alone, this research investigates whether implementing SHLMAS can produce measurable improvements in library circulation compared to the school's existing manual process.

The findings of this study may serve as a reference for educational institutions seeking affordable and scalable approaches to modernizing library operations through workflow automation.
